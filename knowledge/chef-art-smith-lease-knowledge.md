## Executive Summary

This 10-year restaurant lease, commencing May 11, 2020, establishes Chef Art Smith's Reunion at Navy Pier in a 3,200 square foot ground-floor location (Suite 300). The lease provides strong tenant protections with two 5-year renewal options (potential 20-year total occupancy through 2040). The restaurant specializes in Southern cuisine, aligning with Chef Art Smith's culinary brand. Tenant pays base rent (amounts in Exhibit M), CAM, Marketing Fee, and percentage rent on sales exceeding annual breakpoints. The lease includes comprehensive repair and maintenance obligations typical of full-service restaurant operations, with Tenant responsible for all interior maintenance, equipment, and specialized services (grease trap, hood cleaning, pest control). Assignment requires Landlord consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), but Tenant remains liable unless expressly released. Sales reporting is required monthly (within 15 days) with significant penalties for late submission ($500 + $100/day). The lease provides long-term stability in a prime Navy Pier location with standard restaurant lease provisions and two renewal options providing potential occupancy through 2040.

### When is Base Rental Due?

**Answer:**

**Payment Schedule:**
- Due monthly in advance
- Due on the 1st day of each month
- First payment due May 1, 2020 (prior to Commencement)

**Payment Method:**
- Delivered to Landlord's designated address
- Via check, wire transfer, or ACH
- Without demand, setoff, or deduction

**Late Payment Penalties:**
- **Grace period:** 5 days
- **Late charge:** 5% of overdue amount if not paid by 6th of month
- **Interest:** 1.5% per month (18% per annum) on amounts unpaid after 10 days

**Source:** Section 5.1 (Page 8-9)

**Exact Language:** "Base Rent shall be due and payable in advance on the first day of each month during the Term, without demand, setoff, or deduction. If Base Rent is not paid within five (5) days of the due date, Tenant shall pay a late charge equal to five percent (5%) of the overdue amount."

**Interpretation:** Monthly advance payment is standard. The 5-day grace period provides minimal flexibility. Late charges and interest accumulate quickly after the grace period.

### Penalty for Late Sales Reporting

**Answer:**

**Late Sales Report Penalties:**

**Initial Penalty:**
- $500 for first 10 days late
- Applies per reporting period (monthly)

**Escalating Penalty:**
- Additional $100 per day after initial 10-day period
- No cap specified on daily penalties

**Additional Consequences:**
- Landlord may estimate percentage rent and bill Tenant
- Tenant loses right to dispute estimated amount if more than 30 days late
- Repeated violations (3+ in 12 months) constitute material default
- May trigger audit rights at Tenant's expense

**Example Calculation:**
- 25 days late = $500 (first 10 days) + $1,500 (15 days × $100) = **$2,000 total penalty**

**Source:** Section 5.3, Section 5.4, Section 16.1 (Page 11, Page 12, Page 28)

**Exact Language:** "Tenant shall provide Landlord with a written statement of Gross Sales within fifteen (15) days after the end of each calendar month. If Tenant fails to timely provide such statement, Tenant shall pay to Landlord as a penalty (and not as liquidated damages) the sum of Five Hundred Dollars ($500) for the first ten (10) days of delinquency, and an additional One Hundred Dollars ($100) per day for each day thereafter that such statement is not provided. Landlord may, in its sole discretion, estimate the Gross Sales and bill Tenant accordingly, which estimate shall be binding if Tenant fails to provide actual sales figures within thirty (30) days of billing."

**Critical Notes:**
- The penalty structure is punitive and escalating to ensure timely compliance
- The binding estimate provision after 30 days shifts risk to Tenant
- Repeated failures may lead to lease termination

**⚠️ Review Required:** The penalty amounts can become very large. Tenant should implement automated reporting systems.

## Article IX - Repairs

### Landlord's Responsibility to Repair

**Answer:**

**Landlord's Structural & Core Repair Obligations:**

**Structural Systems:**
- Foundation and structural frame
- Load-bearing walls and columns
- Roof structure and membrane (but not interior damage from leaks)
- Exterior walls and windows

**Building Systems:**
- Base building HVAC (up to Premises demising walls)
- Main electrical service to building
- Plumbing risers and mains
- Fire protection systems (sprinklers, alarms)
- Elevators and common area systems

**Common Areas:**
- Lobbies, hallways, restrooms
- Parking areas and walkways
- Landscaping and exterior lighting
- Snow removal and exterior maintenance

**Important Limitations:**
- ❌ Landlord NOT responsible if damage caused by Tenant
- ❌ Interior premises repairs are Tenant's responsibility
- ❌ Tenant modifications/improvements excluded
- ⚠️ Landlord has "reasonable time" to complete repairs (not defined)
- ⚠️ No rent abatement unless Premises rendered unusable for 30+ consecutive days

**Source:** Section 7.1, Section 7.3, Section 9.2 (Page 14, Page 15, Page 19)

**Exact Language:** "Landlord shall maintain and repair the structural portions of the Building, including the foundation, structural frame, load-bearing walls, roof structure, exterior walls, and base building mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems serving the Building generally. Landlord shall also maintain all Common Areas in good condition and repair. Notwithstanding the foregoing, Landlord shall have no obligation to repair any damage caused by the negligence or willful misconduct of Tenant, its employees, agents, contractors, or invitees, and Tenant shall reimburse Landlord for the cost of any such repairs within thirty (30) days of invoice."

**Caveats:**
- The term "reasonable time" for repairs is undefined and subject to interpretation
- What constitutes "structural" versus "non-structural" may be disputed
- Tenant should document all repair requests in writing
