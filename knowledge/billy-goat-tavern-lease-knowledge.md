## Executive Summary

This 10-year restaurant lease, commencing June 1, 2022, establishes Billy Goat Tavern's presence at Navy Pier in a 2,850 square foot ground-floor location. The lease provides favorable terms for an iconic Chicago brand, with moderate base rent starting at $15,000/month ($63/sq ft annually) and escalating 5% every two years. In addition to base rent, Tenant pays CAM ($2,375/month), Marketing Fee ($1,188/month), and percentage rent (6% of sales over $3.7M annually). The lease includes one 5-year renewal option, exercisable with 180 days notice. Billy Goat is responsible for all interior maintenance, utilities, and compliance with restaurant-specific requirements (grease traps, hood cleaning, pest control). The permitted use is limited to full-service restaurant and bar operations, aligning with Billy Goat's traditional tavern concept. Assignment requires Landlord consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), but Tenant remains liable even after assignment unless expressly released. Sales reporting is required monthly (within 15 days) with penalties for late submission starting at $250 plus $50/day. The lease provides long-term stability in a prime Navy Pier location with predictable rent escalations and standard restaurant lease provisions.

### Penalty for Late Sales Reporting

**Answer:**

**Late Sales Report Penalties:**

**Initial Penalty:**
- $250 for the first 5 days late
- Applies per report (monthly, quarterly, or annual)

**Escalating Penalty:**
- Additional $50 per day after the first 5 days
- No cap specified on daily penalties

**Additional Consequences:**
- Landlord may estimate Gross Sales and bill Tenant
- Tenant loses right to dispute estimated amount if report is more than 30 days late
- Repeated violations (3+ in any 12-month period) may constitute material default
- May trigger audit rights at Tenant's expense (Landlord can audit immediately)

**Example Calculation:**
- 20 days late = $250 (first 5 days) + $750 (15 days × $50) = **$1,000 total penalty**
- 45 days late = $250 + $2,000 (40 days × $50) = **$2,250 total penalty**

**Source:** Section 5.3, Section 16.1 (Page 10, Page 26)

**Exact Language:** "If Tenant fails to timely provide such sales statement, Tenant shall pay to Landlord as a penalty the sum of Two Hundred Fifty Dollars ($250) for the first five (5) days of delinquency, and an additional Fifty Dollars ($50) per day for each day thereafter. Landlord may estimate Gross Sales and bill Tenant accordingly, which estimate shall be binding if Tenant fails to provide actual sales figures within thirty (30) days."

**Interpretation:** The penalty structure is more modest than Chef Art Smith's lease but still creates financial pressure for timely compliance. The $50/day escalation after the initial 5 days is reasonable but can accumulate quickly. The most significant risk is Landlord's ability to estimate sales and make that estimate binding after 30 days, which could result in overpayment of percentage rent. Three violations in 12 months triggering default status is particularly concerning as it could jeopardize the entire lease.

**Recommendation:** Tenant should implement calendar reminders and automated reporting to avoid penalties. The binding estimate provision after 30 days is particularly problematic and could result in disputes over actual vs. estimated sales.

## Key Risks & Opportunities

### Risks
- Tenant remains liable after assignment unless expressly released - could be on hook if assignee defaults
- No cap on percentage rent if sales significantly exceed breakpoint - could become expensive burden in high-volume years
- Sales reporting penalties accumulate quickly ($250 + $50/day) and repeated violations may constitute default
- Landlord can estimate sales and make binding if Tenant is 30+ days late with reports - risk of overpaying percentage rent
- Renewal rent uncertainty - subject to negotiation or Fair Market Value with only a floor of final year rent
- Biennial 5% rent increases compound over time - Year 10 rent is 21.6% higher than Year 1
- CAM subject to reconciliation - actual costs may exceed budgeted amounts
- Limited control over Marketing Fee expenditures - $14,250-$17,321/year with no guaranteed ROI
- 180-day renewal notice deadline is strict - missing deadline loses renewal rights entirely
- High restaurant operating costs (utilities, equipment, grease trap, hood cleaning) in addition to rent
- Navy Pier foot traffic is highly seasonal - summer peak vs. winter low may create cash flow challenges
- Assignment requires consent even though "not unreasonably withheld" - still creates uncertainty and delay

### Opportunities
- Prime Navy Pier location with 9+ million annual visitors provides strong customer traffic
- Billy Goat brand recognition and Chicago icon status align well with tourist destination
- Moderate starting rent of $63/sq ft is reasonable for Navy Pier prime location
- Predictable 5% biennial rent increases allow for long-term financial planning
- One renewal option provides potential 15-year total occupancy
- Percentage rent only kicks in above $3.7M sales - provides relief if sales are modest
- Permitted transfers to affiliates without consent allows corporate flexibility
- Marketing Fee funds collective Navy Pier promotion benefiting all tenants
- Exclusive restaurant/bar use protects against competing uses in Premises
- No personal guaranty mentioned - corporate entity structure limits liability
- Standard repair obligations - no unusual maintenance burdens beyond typical restaurant operations
- Quarterly percentage rent payment (vs. monthly) improves cash flow management
